Ashford Retail Group — capital structure & recovery analysis

A 2021-vintage LBO left Ashford with leverage the current traffic trend can't service. First-lien lenders are likely to end up owning the business; unsecured noteholders are likely to recover close to nothing.

Capital structure

Total debt$1,240m5.5x

Estimated LTM EBITDA of $225m against total debt of $1.24bn.

Why the company is under stress

Same-store sales have declined for six consecutive quarters as foot traffic shifts online, while the store footprint carries long-dated, largely non-cancellable leases fixed during a stronger period for the business. The combination of shrinking cash generation and LBO-era leverage has left minimal covenant headroom — this is a financial and operational stress case together, not a temporary liquidity issue.

Under either scenario, recovery breaks entirely below the second lien — first-lien lenders are the effective fulcrum security and the natural post-reorg owners.

Likely restructuring path

A prepackaged or pre-negotiated Chapter 11 that converts the first-lien term loan into the majority of new equity, likely with second-lien holders receiving warrants or a small equity stub to secure support, and unsecured notes receiving negligible recovery. Store-count rationalization as part of the plan (lease rejections) is likely, given the fixed-cost pressure described above.

What would break this thesis

This view breaks if same-store sales stabilize to flat-to-positive for two consecutive quarters and the company secures a covenant amendment or maturity extension — either would materially reduce near-term restructuring probability and reprice the second lien higher.

Conclusion

First lien is the fulcrum security across both scenarios modeled; second lien and unsecured are likely impaired in any outcome short of a sustained traffic recovery.
